# The Four-Corner Federated Flow — a walkthrough

## What the diagram shows

The sequence diagram traces a **single message end to end** in the fully federated (four-corner) case: two legal entities — **Entity A** (sender) and **Entity B** (recipient) — served by **different** providers, from name resolution through to a relying party verifying the evidence eighteen months later. It follows the **verification grade** (the default) and shows the **digest-mismatch branch** as the alternative outcome.

![Four-corner federated flow](diagrams/federated-flow.svg)

The diagram is **illustrative only**. The normative flow is the Internet-Draft `draft-sbm-mls-erd` (*Message Flows and Delivery States*) and the TS-shaped `TS-SBM-QERDS-Binding` (clauses 4.1 and 6); the objects, seals and grades it names are defined there and in the umbrella profile. For the *why* behind the evidence objects — the seal-then-timestamp construction, the digest, the delivery grades, the grade commitment — see the companion [`evidence-layer-explainer.md`](evidence-layer-explainer.md); this document narrates the *sequence*.

One caveat to read first: the four-corner **relay evidence** the diagram shows in Phase 4 (`B.1`/`B.2`/`B.3`) is the **profile-2** interoperability target (TS clause 4.1), **not** exercised by the single-provider (profile-1) reference PoC, where the two RDPs are co-located and no inter-provider relay occurs. The diagram is drawn for the federated case precisely to make that relay visible.

## The seven lifelines

The topology is a **four-corner federation**: each entity submits to, and receives evidence from, its own RDP, and the RDPs relay to each other (Phase 4). The exception is group formation: the creating wallet reserves KeyPackages, deposits Welcomes and registers as founder at the *counterparty's* Delivery Service, and collects replies there (Phase 2, and *Where the group is routed* in Phase 4 — review agenda A10). Wallets never connect peer-to-peer — that is what makes delivery asynchronous and the evidence enforceable — but the MLS *keys* are negotiated end to end, so the federation transports only ciphertext.

| Lifeline | Role |
|---|---|
| **WU-S** — sender wallet (Entity A) | Computes the content digest and (for availability grade) the grade commitment; drives resolution, group creation and submission. Never issues evidence. |
| **EDD** — directory | The European Digital Delivery directory (Layer 0): resolves a UID to provider endpoints, the signed capability document, and a pointer to published MLS key material. |
| **MSP(out)** — sender-side Delivery Service | The sender's Messaging Service Provider / MLS Delivery Service. Accepts the octets RDP(out) submits — the acceptance the SE rests on — and routes them to the devices it observed joining the group; issues no evidence. |
| **RDP(out)** — sender-side QERDS | The sender's Registered Delivery Provider: issues Sending Evidence, and in this case composes the Evidence Package. |
| **RDP(in)** — recipient-side QERDS | The recipient's Registered Delivery Provider: issues Delivery / Non-Delivery / Refusal Evidence. |
| **MSP(in)** — recipient-side Delivery Service | The recipient's MSP/DS: holds KeyPackages and the message queue for Entity B's devices, and receives relayed messages from RDP(in) as forwarded submissions. |
| **WU-R** — recipient wallet (Entity B) | Decrypts, recomputes the digest, and produces the recipient confirmation the recipient-side RDP relies on. A trust *participant*, not just an endpoint. |

## Phase 1 — Resolution & discovery

The sender wallet resolves the recipient's UID at the **EDD**, which returns the directory record and the recipient's `med_url` (with a signed, `active` status). The wallet then fetches the recipient's sealed discovery documents — **BW-MED** (provider/transport capability), **BW-ORG** (roles, acceptance policy, and the per-content-class `delivery_grades` map) and **BW-MEMBER** (each member's devices, their capabilities and published confirmation keys) — from the recipient's provider, learning its `ds_url`, its RDP, the acceptance policy, and the declared delivery grades. A member is identified by `(UID, MID)` and a device by `(UID, MID, device_id)`: MIDs are unique only within an entity and device labels only within a member, so no step below uses a label on its own.

Everything here is signed or sealed: discovery is fail-closed, and the wallet verifies each document against the trust material before using it. Nothing legal has happened yet — this is pure Layer-0 addressing and key discovery.

## Phase 2 — First contact (MLS group, E2EE)

The sender wallet **reserves** one KeyPackage per recipient device at the recipient's Delivery Service (`POST /keypackages/{uid}/reservations`, with an `Idempotency-Key`), receives each package with its `keypackage_ref`, and **commits** the reservation (`POST /reservations/{reservation_id}/commit`). From commit the packages are **consumed**, whatever happens next: a failed or partial deposit releases nothing, and the creator reserves afresh. It then **creates the MLS group**, adds both parties' devices as leaves, and Commits; the MLS handshake *is* the cryptographic mutual authentication, anchored in qualified trust material and validated against the EU Trusted Lists.

The wallet deposits **one Welcome per device** (`POST /welcome`), each naming its reservation, the consumed `keypackage_ref`, the offered suite and the `group_info_commitment`. Each recipient device collects **its own** queue (`GET /welcome`, device-authenticated), decrypts its Welcome, recomputes the GroupInfo commitment from the item, and checks the offered suite against its published floor. If both hold it **acknowledges** (`DELETE /welcome/{welcome_id}`), which is joining — and which is what keeps it routed: an open invitation places the device in the group only until the Welcome expires, an acknowledged one for good. If either fails it **refuses** (`POST /welcome/{welcome_id}/refusal`, reason `group-info-mismatch` or `suite-below-published-floor`, authenticated by the KeyPackage it holds): the Welcome leaves its queue, and the creator collects a typed outcome naming the refusing member (`GET /group-establishment/outcomes`). A Welcome whose window closes unacknowledged is no longer served and stops placing the device in the group; it creates no outcome. A device may hold more than one invitation into a group — re-reserving produces exactly that — and each invitation owns only its own claim. Having deposited, the creating device registers itself as the group's **founder** (`POST /groups/{group_id}/founder`, device-authenticated). MLS begins a group with its creator and adds no Welcome for it, so without this the Delivery Service would route the counterparty's reply to every device except the one that created the group. Because KeyPackages are pre-published this works **asynchronously**: the recipient need not be online at send time. From here the channel exists and carries only ciphertext.

## Phase 3 — Send (content stays end-to-end encrypted)

The sender wallet builds the **application envelope**, computes the **`payload_hash`** over the plaintext — `raw-sha256` over the transmitted octets, or `manifest-sha256` over the deterministic-CBOR manifest of a multipart payload — and encrypts the envelope + payload as an MLS **PrivateMessage**. It signs the submission tuple and submits to **RDP(out)** (`POST /submissions`, wallet-RDP): the exact MLSMessage octets together with the metadata only the sender knows (`payload_hash`, the MLS session, `sent_at` and `expires_at`, `auth_method`), inside an **authenticated** submission session. It does not submit to MSP(out): the provider that will attest the submission is the one that receives it.

Before anything moves, RDP(out) validates the request, the sender's identity and signature, the acceptance policy the submission pins, and the expiry — a non-positive TTL, one above the recipient's declared maximum, or a `sent_at` more than five minutes ahead of its own clock is refused with nothing stored. It then hands **the same octets** to the Delivery Service (`POST /messages`, provider-authenticated). The DS computes the digest itself, returns an acceptance record, and creates one delivery item per device it observed joining the group — both entities, each item bound to its own member. In this flow MSP(out) observed none: every invitation was deposited at MSP(in), so this acceptance creates no items and exists for what the SE rests on (see *Where the group is routed*, below).

**RDP(out)** verifies that authenticated sender session — the MLS credential chains to a Trusted List, is unexpired and unrevoked, and its UID matches `sender_uid` — and issues **Sending Evidence (SE)**, the `A.1-SubmissionAcceptance` event, **sealed** and carrying a **qualified timestamp**. The SE is the anchor for sender attribution (Article 44(1)(b)), and by default it carries the sender's own signature too: a `sender_confirmation` by the sending device over the exact submission tuple (`origin_proof` `sender-signed`), beside the RDP's record of the authenticated session. It is sealed only AFTER the Delivery Service has accepted the same octets — sealing first is prohibited, so the SE never attests bytes the DS never received. The digest is the hinge — it lets the SE refer to the content the provider never sees.

## Phase 4 — Four-corner relay (ciphertext + evidence)

This is the phase that only exists in the federated case, and it runs **between the two RDPs**. The MSPs are each side's local delivery service and do not relay to each other: an MSP-to-MSP relay would put a participant that need not be qualified on the evidence-bearing path, and it is rejected.

**RDP(out)** relays **the same octets together with the sealed SE** to **RDP(in)** (`POST /relay/messages`). RDP(in) verifies the SE's seal and recomputes the digest of the octets it received against the SE's `envelope_hash`, refusing a hop whose bytes differ. It then hands the octets to **its** Delivery Service as a **forwarded submission** (`POST /messages` with `origin`: the originating RDP and its sealed SE). MSP(in) keys the message on the **origin's** namespace, `(RDP(out), message_id)`, only because that SE proves it; it records RDP(in) as the forwarder, and creates one item per device it observed joining — B's devices, and the group's founder. Two origins that use the same local `message_id` therefore stay two messages at MSP(in), and an origin claimed without a verifying SE is refused. RDP(in) returns the hop result (`POST /relay/messages/{message_id}/result`): `B.1-RelayAcceptance`, or `B.2-RelayRejection` with a typed reason; RDP(out) issues `B.3-RelayFailure` if the relay cannot be achieved. Each hop's evidence is sealed by its issuer and carries a **qualified timestamp at that hop**.

Before exchanging anything, the RDPs authenticate each other against the EU Trusted Lists **and** the federation membership register. The register half is a **live decision** — a lease taken once, at the start of the exchange, on the deciding RDP's own clock, against a fresh `admitted` assertion. It authorises the exchange; it is not evidence that either RDP was admitted at any particular act, which is verified later, act by act, against a record asserted after it (Phase 7).

**Where the group is routed.** A Delivery Service routes the devices it observed joining: the founder registered with it, and the devices invited through it. Here the creator deposits every invitation, and registers as founder, at MSP(in), because that is where B's KeyPackages are — so MSP(in) routes the whole group, and a reply from B reaches the creator's device through MSP(in). Whether each entity's devices should instead be served by its own Delivery Service, and how a removal from the MLS group after formation reaches a Delivery Service's delivery set, is open (review agenda A10).

Two things to keep straight here. First, the providers move only **ciphertext and evidence** — never plaintext. Second, this relay leg and its `B.x` evidence are the **profile-2 four-corner target** (TS clause 4.1): they are normative *for that profile*, to be confirmed by a conformity assessment body, and are **not** present in the single-provider profile-1 deployment, where RDP(out) and RDP(in) are the same operator and no relay occurs.

## Phase 5 — Deliver & decrypt (states S1 → S2, then the digest gate)

The message reaches the recipient side. The profile distinguishes four recipient-side **states**: **S1** (queued for the recipient's devices at MSP(in)), **S2** (the **acknowledged handover** — the device collected the bytes in an authenticated session *and* acknowledged them), **S3** (decrypted and **digest-verified** by an authorised wallet), and **S4** (**accepted** under the entity's published policy). S2 is two operations, not one: the device **collects** (`GET /messages`, device-authenticated — the item becomes `transferred` and carries a collection token) and **acknowledges** (`POST /messages/{issuing_rdp_id}/{message_id}/receipt-ack`, quoting the token). The DS then signs a **receipt** whose `server_time` it observed itself; a queue that merely holds the message is not S2. The wallet **decrypts** and **recomputes `payload_hash`**. At the verification and acceptance grades S2 does not by itself establish delivery.

*Open, and drawn as open in the diagram:* the DE issuer must verify that receipt, and no published operation carries it to any RDP. Which RDP issues the DE in the four-corner case is also unsettled (review agenda A1).

Then comes the **digest gate**, the diagram's `alt` branch:

**If the digest matches (S3).** The recipient wallet delivers a **recipient confirmation** to RDP(in) (`POST /confirmations`, `confirmation_kind` `s3`, naming the originating provider and the `message_id`), bound to `(message_id, MLS session, policy_ref, payload_hash, result)`. The authenticated principal must be the confirming member — and device, where one is named — of the SE's recipient entity, and that is decided before any stored answer is served. Before relying on it, **RDP(in)** performs the checks the fifteenth review made normative (INTF-1..3), before anything is stored or sealed: it verifies the **confirmation's authenticity** (a recipient wallet signature over the **deterministic-CBOR encoding** of the confirmation object, *or* — equally admissible — delivery within a recipient session authenticated to the wallet and cryptographically bound to the confirming member; INTF-1); that the confirming `(mid, device_id)` is an **active, acknowledgement-capable member** of the SE's recipient (INTF-2); and that the confirmation's **`policy_ref` and MLS session equal the SE's** (INTF-3). A confirmation is **one member's act**: an exact retry converges, the same member making a different claim is refused, and distinct eligible members each count once. RDP(in) evaluates the **acceptance policy** the SE pinned — for `any-one` the first eligible confirmation satisfies it, so S4 coincides with S3; for `quorum:<n>` or `all` it is the n-th or the last — and issues **Delivery Evidence (DE)** (`E.1-ContentHandover` here, or `C.3-ConsignmentAcceptance` under quorum/all), sealed and time-stamped, **embedding the confirmation as `s3_attestation`**. Its `delivered_at` is the instant **RDP(in) received and verified the confirmation that completed the policy**, on its own clock — not the S2 receipt, and not the wallet's `verified_at` — so every act the DE rests on precedes it, and a confirmation received after `expires_at` is late whatever it says. The DE carries the same `payload_hash` as the SE, binding delivered content to sent content.

> The diagram draws the wallet-signed variant of the confirmation for concreteness; the session-authenticated variant is equally valid (INTF-1). A self-asserted `session_authenticated` flag on a channel that is *not* authenticated and member-bound is rejected — it satisfies no acceptance policy.

**If the digest does not match.** The recipient wallet does not confirm a match. It delivers a **mismatch confirmation** — its own proof, wallet-signed or member-session-bound, carrying the digest it recomputed (`POST /confirmations`, `confirmation_kind` `mismatch`). RDP(in) verifies it as it verifies any confirmation and issues **Non-Delivery Evidence (NDE)** with event `D.2-ContentConsignmentFailure` and reason `payload-hash-mismatch`, **embedding that proof**. Silence is never a mismatch: a device that sends nothing has proved nothing about the digest, and without a confirmation the outcome at expiry is an NDE `expired`. The digest is message-wide, so a verified mismatch ends the message; confirmations arriving afterwards change nothing. This is a **first-class failure**, not ordinary non-delivery: a mismatch may indicate corruption, an implementation error, or an attack, and it carries audit obligations. The NDE is relayed back to the sender side for the package.

The other delivery grades are not drawn but map onto the same states: the **acceptance grade** issues its DE at S4 and is dated by the completing member's confirmation; the **availability grade** — for content classes the recipient has expressly declared — issues its DE at the acknowledged handover, is dated by the DS receipt's `server_time` (the only grade that date belongs to), carries no confirmation object, and instead carries a verifiable **grade commitment**. See [`evidence-layer-explainer.md`](evidence-layer-explainer.md) §§6–7.

## Phase 6 — Evidence Package

The EP authority — **RDP(out)** in the single-provider composition role — composes the **Evidence Package (EP)**: the `se`, the `outcome` (the DE, or the NDE/RE), and the **`rdp_chain`** listing *both* RDPs (`RDP(out)`, `RDP(in)`) with references to the per-hop evidence, sealed and time-stamped at the moment of composition — and composing is an act, so the composer must be admitted to the federation at the instant that timestamp attests. The EP is the **wallet-storable, independently verifiable artefact** each party keeps: RDP(out) returns it to the sender wallet, and the recipient side holds its EP/DE. It is composed once an outcome exists, so it always carries at least one outcome.

## Phase 7 — Verification (later, by any relying party)

Any relying party — here, a wallet eighteen months on — verifies the package **decode-then-validate**: **decode** each object's COSE_Sign1 deterministic-CBOR payload — the authoritative artefact — and verify the artefact's **COSE_Sign1** advanced electronic seal (its first element) over those bytes against the RDP's **QSealC** chained to the **EU Trusted Lists**, verify the **qualified timestamp** (the second artefact element) over `SHA-256` of the first, and check that the **`payload_hash`** in the evidence equals the digest of the plaintext the party actually holds. Certificate and credential validity are evaluated **at evidence time, not at verification time**, and so is federation admission: every provider the evidence names must be admitted at the instant of **its own** act, according to a Federation Authority record that was authenticated and asserted after that act. Qualification and admission are independent gates; neither implies the other.

These are the checks the profile designs the **Article 43(2)** presumptions — integrity, sender, addressee, time — to rest on. Whether and how the presumptions attach is a legal question this study does not answer (review agenda, *Legal*). Throughout the entire sequence, **no provider ever saw the content**: the property the whole profile exists to demonstrate.

## What the diagram deliberately leaves out

- **One grade, one message.** It follows the verification-grade path for a single message. Acceptance and availability grades, quorum acceptance, confidentiality scopes, multipart manifests and Change-Indication Evidence are out of frame; the object detail behind each step is in [`evidence-layer-explainer.md`](evidence-layer-explainer.md).
- **The confirmation variant.** It draws the wallet-signed confirmation; the member-bound session-authenticated variant is equally admissible (INTF-1).
- **Profile boundary.** The `B.1`/`B.2`/`B.3` relay evidence is the profile-2 four-corner target (TS clause 4.1), not part of the co-located profile-1 deployment.
- **Retries and idempotency.** A sender may retry until `ttl` expires; `message_id` is globally unique per issuing environment, so an exact retry is idempotent while a collision is rejected at intake (INTF-4). Not drawn, to keep the happy path legible.
