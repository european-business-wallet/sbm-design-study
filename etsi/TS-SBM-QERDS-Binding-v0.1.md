## 1 Scope

The present document defines the binding of Electronic Registered Delivery (ERD) messages and evidence — whose semantics are defined in ETSI EN 319 522-2 and whose qualified-service requirements are set by Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 Article 44 and Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1944 — to the MLS-based secure-messaging transport **SM-MLS**, whose wire protocol is defined in the companion Internet-Draft `draft-sbm-mls-erd` [I-D].

It specifies the qualified-electronic-registered-delivery-service (QERDS) conformance layer over SM-MLS: the evidence seal and secure cryptographic device, cryptography per the Agreed Cryptographic Mechanisms (ACM), identity proofing and authentication binding with recipient-identification-before-delivery gating, change indication, the ETSI EN 319 522 event and evidence-semantics mapping, the reason-code mapping, the pilot and production conformance profiles, the compliance with Article 44 and CIR (EU) 2025/1944, and an Implementation Conformance Statement (ICS) pro forma.

SM-MLS is a **new binding** in the sense of ETSI EN 319 522-4: it binds ERD messages and evidence to MLS-over-MSP transport, which is neither the AS4 binding (EN 319 522-4-1 clause 5) nor the RFC 5322 binding (clause 6).

## 4 SM-MLS binding — general concepts

Per REQ-ERDS-5.4.1-06 the RDP shall generate evidence for the ERD events defined in ETSI EN 319 522-1 [2] clause 6, and per REQ-ERDS-5.4.1-08 that evidence shall comply with the evidence semantics of ETSI EN 319 522-2 [3] clause 8. Clause 8 provides the normative mapping.

The conformity strategy for this new binding is:

1. **Standards-based presumption (Article 44(1a)).** SM-MLS preserves the EN 319 522-2 evidence semantics (clause 8), so the presumption of compliance rests on the semantic layer even though the transport binding is new.
2. **CAB confirmation (Article 44(2a)).** Interoperability between QERDSPs and the SM-MLS profile itself shall be confirmed by a conformity assessment body.
3. **Cross-provider relay — scoped claim.** Where the sender-side and recipient-side RDPs differ, the present document's four-corner relay claim is scoped to **homogeneous SM-MLS federations**: both RDPs implement this profile, and the B.x relay evidence objects, the reason × stage × event table (clause 8.1) and the relay machinery of clause 8 ARE the interchange format — no external format mapping is involved. The four-corner homogeneous case is specified normatively and machine-checked, and remains the interoperability target to be CAB-confirmed; a deployment shall not claim four-corner QERDS interoperability until that confirmation is obtained. **Heterogeneous interoperation — an SM-MLS federation exchanging with a different QERDS implementation via the EN 319 522-2 Common Services Interface (clause 9) and the EN 319 522-3 formats — is NOT claimed by this document**: it requires a normative mapping/gateway annex that does not yet exist, whose named prerequisites are (i) issuer attribution across the gateway boundary, (ii) event/reason translation (the clause 8.1 registry's EN 319 522-2 external-mapping column is the seed), (iii) qualified-timestamp preservation across the format transformation, and (iv) CE issuance for the transformation itself under the observable-bytes model (CE, clause 4). A deployment shall not claim any combination beyond its row in the claim matrix below.

**Interoperability claim matrix (normative).** Exactly one status per combination; claims beyond a row's status are non-conformant:

| Combination | Status |
|---|---|
| Single-provider SM-MLS (one RDP, both directions) | **Implemented** — specified normatively; reference-tooling-verified (`make conformance`) |
| Four-corner homogeneous SM-MLS (two RDPs, both this profile) | **Specified + machine-checked; CAB-confirmation pending** — clause 8 relay evidence, LINT-RLY/BND relay rules; no interoperability claim until CAB confirmation |
| Heterogeneous via CSI / EN 319 522-3 gateway (SM-MLS ↔ another QERDS) | **Future work — NOT claimed** — requires the mapping/gateway annex (prerequisites i–iv above) |

Both the sender-side and recipient-side RDP shall be QERDS-qualified. The MSP may be non-qualified; colocation of the MSP and RDP does not change the evidence semantics.

### 4.1 Four-corner relay requirements (deployment profile 2, normative)

Item 3 above names the target; the present clause states what a four-corner deployment shall satisfy **beyond** the single-provider case. The HTTP contracts realising these requirements remain companion deliverables (the I-D, *Deployment-Defined Interfaces*); the requirements below are normative now, and the profile-2 conformance claim depends on all of them plus the CAB confirmation of item 2.

**Relay evidence set and issuance duties.** The B.x relay events shall be evidenced as follows:

| Event | Evidence | Issuer | Trigger | Returned to | QTS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B.1 RelayAcceptance | relay-acceptance evidence (profile-2 object set) | **recipient-side RDP** | accepting the relayed message over the relay interface | sender-side RDP (EP `rdp_chain[]`) | shall, at the hop |
| B.2 RelayRejection | relay-rejection evidence with a typed reason | **recipient-side RDP** | refusing the relay (unknown/suspended UID, policy, malformed) | sender-side RDP, which shall issue the sender-facing NDE | shall, at the hop |
| B.3 RelayFailure | NDE `B.3-RelayFailure` (`routing-failed`) | **sender-side RDP** | relay unachievable within the retry policy | sender | shall (clause 8.1) |

The sender-side RDP shall issue SE, perform submission gating, issue B.3, and compose the EP aggregating the recipient-side evidence; the recipient-side RDP shall issue B.1/B.2 and all consignment-side evidence (DE at the declared delivery grade, NDE, RE) under the recipient gating of clause 6. Each hop's evidence shall be sealed by its issuer and carry a qualified timestamp **at that hop**; the EP `rdp_chain[]` shall list both RDPs and reference the per-hop evidence.

**Dispute chain.** Every hop shall be independently verifiable: the sender-side RDP's evidence establishes submission and relay dispatch; the recipient-side RDP's evidence establishes relay acceptance (or rejection) and consignment. Both RDPs shall retain their evidence per REQ-ERDS-5.4.1-07, and shall authenticate the peer RDP against the EU Trusted Lists **and** the federation membership register (umbrella §13.1) before exchanging messages or evidence. Failure modes and their evidence: relay timeout ⇒ B.3 by the sender-side RDP; relay rejection ⇒ B.2 by the recipient-side RDP, relayed back and surfaced to the sender as NDE; post-B.1 recipient-side failure ⇒ the D.2 family by the recipient-side RDP, relayed back for EP composition.

**Companion contracts and their required properties.** Profile 2 requires the following interfaces to be normatively contracted (OpenAPI, in the style of the EDD resolver contract); their required properties are normative now:

- **wallet↔RDP** — mutual authentication at the clause 6 assurance levels; global `message_id` uniqueness and idempotent submission ([I-D], *Error Handling and Retries*; INTF-4); integrity-protected confirmation delivery, whose binding requirements are the recipient-confirmation properties of clause 6 (INTF-1 confirmation authentication, INTF-2 roster binding, INTF-3 policy-ref match); authorised evidence retrieval. INTF-1..4 are the clause 4.1 required properties for this surface.
- **Delivery-Service surface** — authenticated KeyPackage publication and replenishment; Welcome queuing integrity; handshake-message ordering per group.
- **RDP↔RDP relay** — an EN 319 522-4-1-compatible binding or a profiled equivalent. The **peer-authentication obligation** is normative now (it does not wait for the companion contract): before any message or evidence is exchanged, each RDP shall **mutually authenticate** the peer, resolve the peer identity to a **Trusted-List QSealC** *and* a **federation membership-register** entry (both gates, umbrella §13.1), and **fail closed** if either gate is unmet; the relay shall provide **replay protection** and evidence-exchange semantics for B.x and the consignment-side objects. The **wire binding that carries these properties** — mutual-TLS chained to the QSealC, an application-layer COSE handshake, or another profiled equivalent — is the profile-2 companion contract and is not fixed here; a resolvable **QSealC channel binding** is required at the **production** profile (clause 9). The reference pilot realises the obligation with a fail-closed application-layer COSE check against the demo trust store — a valid profile-2 realisation; real mTLS/QSealC channel binding is the production target.
- **wallet↔agent** (agent profile, umbrella Annex R) — the controlled interface through which a system member (agent) instructs its wallet: the agent authenticated to the wallet in a session bound to the system member; **key isolation** (the agent cannot extract MLS/seal keys); **attributability** of every instruction to the system member; and **mandate-scope enforcement** at the interface (the wallet refuses an instruction outside the agent's mandate scope). **Profile-5 cross-deployment interoperability requires this contract to be normatively defined** (as profile 2 requires wallet↔RDP); an informative outline is `docs/wallet-agent-interface.md`. An agent-profile (profile-5) companion deliverable.

**Relay retry and failure (normative).** A four-corner relay shall apply a **bounded retry policy with a finite deadline**; retries shall be **idempotent**, keyed by `message_id` (the I-D, *Error Handling and Retries*), so a retried relay never opens a second delivery or issues duplicate evidence. On **deadline exhaustion** the sender-side RDP shall issue **B.3 RelayFailure** and the sender-facing NDE `routing-failed` (clause 8.1); the relay shall **not** block indefinitely. The specific attempt count, backoff and deadline are **deployment-defined**: a single attempt with a short deadline conforms, provided the failure resolves deterministically to B.3. This bounds the failure behaviour without over-fitting a transport.

The B.x relay evidence **object schema** is published: `schemas/evidence-relay.schema.json` (`RelayEvidence-v1`) shall be used for the B.1 RelayAcceptance and B.2 RelayRejection objects, so that a four-corner deployment's archived relay evidence is schema-checkable (`evidence_lint` LINT-RLY-01/02) rather than validated only by convention. Each object shall carry `message_id`, the sending and receiving RDP identifiers (`sending_rdp_id`, `receiving_rdp_id`), the hop instant (`hop_at`) and a typed reason (B.2 only, `reason`); the issuer's seal and the hop qualified timestamp are the object's **artefact envelope** — the wire form is `[cose-sign1, qualified-timestamp]` ([I-D], *Evidence Objects and COSE Packaging*), not in-body fields. B.3 RelayFailure is not a RelayEvidence object — it is the sender-side NDE `routing-failed` (clause 8.1). Publishing the schema does not by itself confer the four-corner interoperability claim, which still depends on the CAB confirmation of clause 4 item 2.

**The per-hop seal digest — what is hashed (normative).** The EP `rdp_chain[].evidence.seal_digest` binds a hop to its relay-evidence object. Its input **shall** be the **first element of the referenced object's evidence artefact** — the exact serialized COSE_Sign1 octets (there is no in-body seal path; the artefact is `[cose-sign1, qualified-timestamp]`):

> `seal_digest.hex` = lowercase-hex( SHA-256( cose-sign1 ) ), where `cose-sign1` is the referenced RelayEvidence artefact's first element as transmitted, with `seal_digest.alg` = `SHA-256`.

The seal travels as a **base64 string**, so "the SHA-256 of the seal" admits two readings — over the decoded bytes, or over the base64 text — and they yield **different digests**. Both satisfy a shape check, so an Evidence Package built under one reading is **unverifiable** by a verifier recomputing the other, while both implementations remain formally conformant: exactly the four-corner dispute chain this reference exists to support would break. The input is therefore fixed here. The **decoded bytes** are chosen because they are already this profile's convention: the qualified-timestamp imprint over a seal (clause 8; `evidence_lint` LINT-PKG-08) digests the same input, so a seal has **exactly one digest** throughout. `alg` names the hash *function*; this clause names its *input*. A verifier holding both the Evidence Package and the referenced object **shall** recompute the digest and reject a mismatch (`bundle_lint` LINT-BND-20); `samples/sample-EP-federated.json` publishes a worked two-hop reference so an implementer can check against bytes rather than prose.

**B.2 rejection → sender NDE reason and event (normative).** When the recipient-side RDP issues a B.2 RelayRejection and it is relayed back, the sender-side RDP shall surface it to the sender as an NDE whose `reason` preserves the typed rejection — it shall **not** collapse every rejection to a single sender-facing reason — and whose `event` is **`B.3-RelayFailure`**: the rejection occurred at the **relay stage**, after the sender-side A.1 SubmissionAcceptance, so exporting it as `A.2-SubmissionRejection` (pre-submission) would make the same submission read as both accepted and rejected before submission — chronologically invalid when the EP is read alone. `B.3-RelayFailure` is the **selected mapping, documented as a translation** where the native B.2 has no exact EN 319 522-2 counterpart; **it is recorded here as pending standards-owner review (ETSI TC ESI)** — the translation rule, not the machinery, is the item under review. The mapping (`bundle_lint` LINT-BND-19 binds the reasons; **LINT-BND-31** binds the event; each value is an existing NDE reason code, so no reason-code registration is required):

| B.2 RelayRejection `reason` | sender-facing NDE `reason` | NDE `event` |
|---|---|---|
| `unknown-uid` | `unknown-uid` | `B.3-RelayFailure` |
| `uid-suspended` | `uid-suspended` | `B.3-RelayFailure` |
| `policy-violation` | `policy-block` | `B.3-RelayFailure` |
| `malformed` | `malformed-envelope` | `B.3-RelayFailure` |

A relay that is unreachable within the retry policy (not a B.2 rejection) is the B.3 RelayFailure case: the sender-side RDP issues NDE `routing-failed` (clause 8.1).

## 5 Evidence seal, secure cryptographic device and cryptography

### 5.1 Advanced electronic seal (Article 44(1)(d))

The COSE_Sign1 signature that an RDP applies to each evidence object (and to the EP) constitutes an advanced electronic seal of the qualified trust service provider within the meaning of Article 44(1)(d). The seal shall be supported by a Qualified Electronic Seal Certificate (QSealC) issued to the RDP and chaining to the Trusted List entry under which the RDP is inscribed as a QERDS. The seal is the COSE_Sign1 over the deterministic-CBOR (RFC 8949 §4.2) encoding of the evidence **body** as defined in [I-D] (there is no in-body seal field to exclude); the wire form is the evidence **artefact** `[cose-sign1, qualified-timestamp]`, whose second element is the qualified timestamp imprinting `SHA-256` of the first (the immutable inner COSE) — a sibling of the seal, not a signed field. In the **production** profile (clause 9) the seal shall additionally embed or reference the QSealC chain in the COSE header (`x5chain`/label 33, or `x5t`), so that a verifier can resolve the signing identity and confirm it is consistent with `rdp_id` and `rdp_chain`; the qualified timestamp shall carry a resolvable TSA identifier; and no demonstration identifiers shall appear.

### 5.2 Secure cryptographic device (REQ-ERDSP-7.5-03)

The RDP evidence-signing private key shall be held and used within a secure cryptographic device that is a trustworthy system certified in accordance with one of: (a) Common Criteria / ISO/IEC 15408 to EAL 4 or higher; (b) the EUCC scheme to EAL 4 or higher; or (c) until 31 December 2030, FIPS PUB 140-3 level 3.

### 5.3 Cryptography per the ACM (REQ-ERDS-7.5-01A, REQ-ERDS-5.1.1-01)

All cryptographic techniques shall be selected in accordance with the ACM [8].

| Use | Mechanism | ACM status |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence seal signature | Ed25519 (EdDSA; COSE alg -8) baseline; ECDSA P-256 (-7)/P-384 (-35) alternative. Implementations shall support Ed25519 | Agreed ("A") |
| MLS key establishment | X25519; ECDH P-256 (DHKEM-P256, the I-D `0x0002` suite); PQ-hybrid X25519+ML-KEM-768 | A / A / Recommended ("R") |
| MLS leaf signature | Ed25519 (EdDSA) baseline; ECDSA P-256 (-7) / P-384 (-35) alternative | A |
| MLS payload AEAD | AES-GCM | A |
| Payload / evidence hashing | SHA-256 (SHA-512 permitted) | A |

The **P-256** MLS suite (I-D `0x0002`) is admitted for **hardware-holdability**: both its key-establishment (ECDH-P256) and its leaf signature (ECDSA-P256, already the agreed seal-signature alternative above) are ACM-agreed, and P-256 is the one curve every commodity secure element holds — so a device on a P-256 secure element can keep its MLS leaf key non-exportable and hardware-backed (MWAP §1). It is not a higher-margin option (that is P-384); it is a *custody* option. ML-KEM shall not be used standalone (ACM note 63); the hybrid suite of [I-D] satisfies this. Transport-layer protocols shall comply with the ACM (REQ-ERDSP-7.8-04).

### 5.4 Long-term validation and archival (REQ-ERDS-5.4.1-07)

The RDP shall archive each evidence object and/or its digest. Evidence that must remain verifiable beyond key rotation or the post-quantum migration should be preserved using an AdES-LTA-style profile: the qualified timestamp is periodically renewed (re-timestamping) over the archived evidence before the protecting certificates or algorithms weaken, so the Article 43(2) presumptions survive long-term.

## 6 Identity proofing and authentication binding

Identity proofing occurs at credential issuance, not at message time. The QTSP that issues the entity's UID attestation / QSealC verifies the entity's identity to the level required by [6] as detailed by [7]; the RDP relies on that proofing. The credential chain that authenticates each MLS leaf node ([I-D]) is the artefact the RDP verifies at submission and delivery.

**Sender (Article 44(1)(b); REQ-QERDS-5.2.1.1-01A, REQ-QERDS-5.2.2-03).** At submission the sender-side RDP shall verify the authenticated sender session — the MLS credential chains to a Trusted List, is unexpired and unrevoked, and its UID matches `sender_uid` — and shall record the resulting `auth_method` in SE.

**Recipient (Article 44(1)(c); REQ-QERDS-5.2.1.1-01, REQ-QERDS-5.2.2-03A, *very high* confidence).** DE issuance shall be gated on an authenticated recipient session meeting REQ-QERDS-5.2.2-03A: the recipient shall be authenticated by an approved `recipient_auth_method` **before** the content is delivered, and that value shall be recorded in DE. Mere device acknowledgement of an MLS PrivateMessage (decryption by a group member) shall not on its own be treated as satisfying Article 44(1)(c). Where recipient identification uses a QERDS-internal process, it shall be conducted in a secured and controlled environment (REQ-QERDS-5.2.1.2-03).

**Three identities.** Three distinct identities are in play — the **legal entity**, the **member** (natural person/system, addressed by a pseudonymous MID or role), and the **device/wallet instance**. Every SE and DE shall carry `auth_context` recording which identity was authenticated, by what `method`, at what level of assurance (`loa`), and under whose `authority`.

**MID accountability.** MIDs are pseudonymous on the wire and evidence carries no personal data. The umbrella BW-MEMBER binding shall nevertheless allow the organisation — and the RDP only under controlled, documented conditions (a dispute or supervisory request) — to resolve a MID to an accountable authorisation event, held off-wire as an `accountability` object (`authorisation_ref`, `retention`, `access_conditions`).

**Agent accountability and non-repudiation (normative, umbrella Annex R).** Where the acting identity is a **system member** (an agent; `auth_context.identity` = system, umbrella §8.4), the evidence chain shall record **who acted** (the acting `mid`, resolvable to the system member's `accountability` event as above) and **within which mandate** (the SE `mandate_ref`, umbrella Annex R). Agent non-repudiation is **layered** (A3): **(1)** the protocol provides **verifiable evidence** that an agent MID acted under a mandate valid at act time — the sealed SE/DE/EP, the acting `mid`, the acted-under `mandate_ref`, and (where opposable) the `mandate_commitment`; **(2)** whether that evidence is **opposable to the entity** is established by the **federation / participation agreement** (the governance instrument, umbrella §13.1), not by this protocol; **(3)** the **final legal qualification** of the act depends on the applicable legal framework. The protocol does not by itself make an agent's act binding on the entity; it makes the act **verifiable**, which is the precondition for the agreement and the law to attach effect. A mandate that was revoked or expired at `sent_at` shall not support acceptance (§ mandate validity; `bundle_lint` LINT-BND-15).

**Mandate verification model (normative, umbrella Annex R / A2).** A relying party verifies an agent's authority as follows. **Issuance.** A mandate may be issued by (i) an **internal organisation authority** — the entity's wallet-administration / mandate authority, whose trust anchor is the entity's QSealC (the same chain that seals its discovery documents); or (ii) an **external issuer** — a QTSP issuing the mandate as a qualified electronic attestation of attributes, whose trust anchor is the issuer's EU Trusted-List entry. `BW-MEMBER.mandate_ref.issuer` names which, and the relying party shall validate the mandate against the corresponding anchor. **Status and revocation.** The relying party shall check the standing mandate's validity window **and** its revocation status — the issuer's status mechanism for an external attestation, or the re-published BW-MEMBER for an internal mandate — and shall evaluate validity **at act time (`sent_at`), not at verification time**, consistent with the credential-validity baseline of this clause (CMP-1/CMP-2). **Conflict rule:** where `BW-MEMBER.mandate_ref` still lists a mandate but the mandate credential was **revoked elsewhere**, revocation wins — evidence produced after the effective revocation shall not establish an in-mandate act. **Binding.** The mandate is issued to the **system member (MID)**; the wallet-agent interface (Annex R.4) enforces its scope; `SE.mandate_ref` records the acted-under mandate and `auth_context.mid` the acting member. **Presentation.** The mandate credential itself is **presented in the end-to-end-encrypted payload** (or resolvable off-wire); only its **reference** (and, where opposable, the commitment) is carried on the evidence — no mandate content leaks on the wire.

NOTE: A concrete minimum wallet assurance baseline — key protection, device binding, confirmation-key lifecycle, compromise-handling latency bounds, build/instance attestation, and the relation to EUDI Wallet certification — is profiled informatively as the **MWAP** (repository: `docs/wallet-assurance-profile.md`); the umbrella deployment profiles (Annex P) reference it as REQUIRED from the production baseline onward.

**Credential compromise and evidence validity (baseline).** Evidence whose wallet-produced confirmation was created while the contributing wallet/device credential was valid — i.e. **before** the credential's suspension or revocation — shall remain **presumptively valid**; that presumption is rebuttable only through the dispute path. Evidence whose wallet-produced confirmation was created **after** the suspension or revocation of the contributing wallet/device credential shall be treated as **invalid**: a verifier shall evaluate certificate and credential validity **at evidence time, not at verification time** (see also the production-verifier architecture note, informative). For a **disputed window** — a claimed compromise predating the formal suspension or revocation — the organisational accountability log (umbrella §8.4) shall be the primary review instrument, and the dispute shall be handled under the governance dispute-resolution clause (umbrella §13.2).

**Federation admission at act time (normative).** Admission to the federation is a gate independent of qualification (umbrella §13.1) and shall be evaluated **at the instant of the act, not at verification time**, consistent with the credential-validity baseline of this clause (CMP-1/CMP-2). For each provider an evidence set names — `rdp_id` on the evidence object, `sending_rdp_id` and `receiving_rdp_id` on relay evidence, and each provider in an Evidence Package's relay chain — the verifier shall resolve that provider's `MembershipRecord` in the federation membership registry over half-open `[from, until)` windows at that act's own instant: `sent_at` for submission, `delivered_at` for delivery, the hop's timestamp for a relay hop, the chain entry's own timestamp for a provider in the relay chain. A record shall be relied on only for acts at or before its `asserted_at`: for a later act it establishes nothing, and admission for that act shall be treated as not established (umbrella §13.1). Composing an Evidence Package is itself an act: the composing provider (the sender-side RDP, `se.rdp_id`) shall be admitted at the instant the package's own qualified timestamp attests, separately from every act the package records; a package whose timestamp yields no readable instant leaves the composer's admission not established. A status of **suspended** or **excluded** at that instant, and a participant to which the registry attributes **no** status at that instant, shall each be treated as admission **not** established. The registry is a **separate verifier input**: a verification carried out without it has not established admission and shall not report that it has.

**Recipient-confirmation binding (wallet↔RDP interface, normative).** A recipient confirmation — the `s3_attestation` on DE-v1, the `recipient_confirmation` on NDE-v1 — is the artefact the RDP relies on to issue confirmation-bearing evidence over the wallet↔RDP interface. The requirements below govern when such a confirmation may be relied upon. They apply to the **confirmation-bearing grades** (verification and acceptance), not to the availability grade, whose DE carries no confirmation object (below) and is therefore not in scope of INTF-1. They presuppose the wallet key-protection and device-binding assurances of the MWAP (above).

[INTF-1] A recipient confirmation relied upon for the issuance of DE-v1 or NDE-v1 shall be authenticated in exactly one of two ways: (a) it carries a recipient wallet advanced electronic signature (`wallet_signature_b64`, a COSE_Sign1 over the confirmation object with the signature field removed, encoded as deterministic CBOR (RFC 8949 §4.2) per the CDDL); or (b) it is delivered within a recipient session that is itself authenticated to the recipient wallet at the assurance level of this clause and that cryptographically binds the calling party to the confirming member, in which case the confirmation carries `session_authenticated` = true. A confirmation carrying neither proof shall be rejected. A `session_authenticated` = true flag asserted over a channel that is not so authenticated and member-bound shall not be accepted and shall not on its own satisfy any acceptance policy. At the wallet↔RDP interface, "binds the calling party to the confirming member" means the session is authenticated by a credential whose subject resolves to the confirmation's `mid` — and, where the confirmation carries one, its `device_id` — for example member-bound mutual-TLS client authentication or a member-bound access token; an entity-level or transport-only session that does not resolve to the confirming member does not satisfy this requirement. The transport binding is a verifier-side duty; as a static floor, a DE relying on the `session_authenticated` variant shall record its recipient session at member- or device-level `auth_context` granularity (`evidence_lint` LINT-DE-12).

[INTF-1a] Where a recipient confirmation carries a wallet advanced electronic signature (variant (a) above), its signing key shall be **resolvable from published discovery**. The confirming device's BW-MEMBER shall publish that device's confirmation-signing key in `devices[].confirmation_key` (BW-MEMBER v1.5, umbrella §8.4); a verifier shall resolve the anchor per (`mid`, `device_id`) and verify `wallet_signature_b64` against it. A confirmation whose signature does not verify against the confirming device's published anchor — or whose device publishes no anchor — shall not be relied upon for the issuance of DE-v1 or NDE-v1. The anchor's `public_key_b64` is the verification key in every profile; at the **production** profile the anchor shall additionally carry an `x5chain` binding the key to the entity QSealC / a Trusted List, so the confirmation signature is an *advanced* electronic signature (clause 5.1) whose certificate validity is evaluated **at evidence time** (CMP-1/CMP-2) — the property that makes the confirmation-key clause of the MWAP (`docs/wallet-assurance-profile.md` §3) satisfiable. Before this anchor existed, BW-MEMBER published only a hash of the *leaf* key, which is neither the confirmation key nor usable to verify a signature, so a wallet-signed confirmation had no verifiable key path (finding D). Statically checkable: `discovery_lint` LINT-DISC-22 (the anchor is present and usable), `bundle_lint` LINT-BND-21 (the signature verifies against it), and `evidence_lint --profile production` LINT-PROD-01/03 on `wallet_signature_b64` (the x5chain identity).

[INTF-1b] The INTF-1/1a construction applies symmetrically to the **sender side** (D4, evidence 2.3). Every SE-v1 shall state its `origin_proof`. Where `origin_proof` = `sender-signed` — the default posture, and REQUIRED for a submission relied upon as an opposable act of the named sender — the SE shall embed a `sender_confirmation`: a wallet advanced electronic signature by the sending member's device (the INTF-1 variant (a) construction) over the full submission tuple — `message_id`, `sender_uid`, `sender_addr`, `recipient_uid`, `recipient_addr`, `payload_hash`, `envelope_hash`, `mls_state`, `acceptance_policy_ref`, `scope_ref`, `sent_at`, `expires_at` — so the sender independently signs the exact transmitted octets, not a paraphrase, and signs WHO the message is addressed to as well as which entity it names (the two addresses became REQUIRED and entered the signed tuple, and this clause went on listing ten fields, so an implementer following the TS signed a tuple the Schema rejects). The signing key shall be resolvable from published discovery exactly as INTF-1a provides, from the **sender** entity's BW-MEMBER per (`mid`, `device_id`), the member active at `sent_at`. Every copied field of the confirmation shall equal the SE's own value; a diverging confirmation shall be rejected. Where `origin_proof` = `provider-attested` — the fallback for constrained senders — the evidence proves that the sending provider attested a submission by an authenticated session of the sender entity; it does **not** prove that the named sender authorised those octets, the sending RDP (or any party holding its seal key) being technically able to fabricate such an attestation, and every claim derived from the SE shall be narrowed accordingly. Statically checkable: `evidence_lint` LINT-DE-19 (tuple coherence + the wallet-signature payload binding), `bundle_lint` LINT-BND-28 (sender-side roster resolution and anchor verification — the LINT-BND-21 mirror). [INTF-2] Before issuing DE-v1 or NDE-v1 from a recipient confirmation, the RDP shall verify that the confirmation's `mid` — and its `device_id` — resolves to a BW-MEMBER of the message's recipient entity (the SE `recipient_uid`) whose `status` is `active` and that holds an `ack`-capable device; the named device shall be that ack-capable device. **For the wallet-signed variant (finding R3) `device_id` is REQUIRED** (schema-enforced on `S3Attestation`/`RecipientConfirmation` where `wallet_signature_b64` is present): the COSE signer shall resolve to *that* device's published `confirmation_key` (INTF-1a), and that device shall be active at the confirmation's `verified_at` — so a retired or compromised device cannot sign a relied-upon confirmation. For the session-authenticated variant `device_id` remains OPTIONAL. For a quorum or all acceptance, every member named in the DE `quorum` array shall likewise resolve to an active, ack-capable member of the recipient entity; a non-resolving acker shall not count toward the quorum. Each quorum entry shall state how its member's act is attested (evidence 2.3): a `wallet-signed` entry is an independently portable per-member proof — the same construction as the INTF-1 variant (a) confirmation, naming its `device_id` and `message_id`, its signature verified against that member's published `confirmation_key` anchor per INTF-1a — while a `provider` entry is an assertion of the recipient's RDP and **explicitly narrows** the acceptance claim for that member; the entry itself carries the narrowing. A session-authenticated confirmation that claims member-grade authentication shall retain a verifiable `session_binding` (a privacy-aware digest of the session token, TLS exporter or session transcript, retained under the §4.3 duty); a bare `session_authenticated` flag with no binding is the provider-attested (narrowed) mode and shall not be treated as member-produced proof. **Refusal binding (extension of INTF-1/INTF-2 to RE-v1, normative, evidence 2.3).** A refusal is a legally operative act of the same rank as an acceptance, and its evidence shall identify **who** refused. Every RE-v1 shall carry `refusal_kind`. Where `refusal_kind` = `member` — an individual recipient member declined the message — the RE shall name the refusing `mid`, record the `auth_context` of the refusing session, and embed a recipient-produced `refusal_confirmation` covering the refused message (`message_id`, `payload_hash`, `refused_at`, `result` = `refused`); that confirmation shall be authenticated exactly as INTF-1 provides for an acceptance confirmation — wallet-signed (variant (a), the key resolved from the member's published `confirmation_key` anchor per INTF-1a) or delivered within a member-bound authenticated session (variant (b)) — and the refusing `mid` shall resolve per INTF-2 to an active BW-MEMBER of the recipient entity as it stood at `refused_at` (the historical read of the as-of duty; refusal requires no `ack` capability — declining is not acknowledging, but it does require membership). An RE asserting `refused-by-user` without such member proof is an RDP-only assertion of a user act and shall be rejected. Where `refusal_kind` = `organisation-policy` — the published acceptance policy refused the message — the RE shall reference the published policy (`acceptance_policy_ref`), shall not name a refusing member, shall not embed a `refusal_confirmation`, and shall not carry a reason that claims or implies a user act. **Acceptance-policy evaluation — the evaluator of record (normative).** RDP(in) shall be the **sole evidence-authoritative evaluator** of the published acceptance policy. The recipient's MSP / Delivery Service collects and forwards member-level confirmation objects and MLS acknowledgement signals; nothing the MSP forwards shall be treated as validated, counted or decided. Before issuing a DE, RDP(in) shall itself: validate every contributing confirmation under INTF-1/1a (and, for a member refusal, the refusal binding above); resolve every contributing member under INTF-2; and evaluate the acceptance policy selected for the message over the resulting set of **distinct active members** (the umbrella §8.3 counting unit — never per leaf or per device). A DE whose acceptance evaluation was performed by any component other than RDP(in), or over unvalidated forwarded signals, is not conformant. The authenticated data the MSP forwards is, since evidence 2.3, the confirmation objects themselves (wallet-signed, or explicitly narrowed provider entries) — the MSP adds no authentication of its own.

**Admissible authentication combinations (normative; floors pilot-provisional).** Every evidence object recording an `auth_context` shall carry an **admissible (identity, method, LoA) tuple** for its context, per the registry `registries/auth-assurance.json` (design-authority-owned; registering a method or adjusting an admissible set is a registry action, umbrella §13.4). The registry is an explicit **tuple table, never a scalar ordering**: a registered method carries the LoA labels it can truthfully claim (a method claiming above its ceiling shall be rejected), and each context — SE submission (split by `origin_proof`), DE confirmation (split by wallet-signed vs session), availability-grade delivery, member refusal, grade dispute — the tuples admissible for it, with **combined-factor elevation** stated where it applies: a wallet advanced electronic signature over the evidence content is a possession factor the session LoA alone does not express, so the sender-signed and wallet-signed arms admit a `substantial` session that the corresponding session-only and provider-attested arms shall not. An evidence object carrying a REGISTERED method outside its admissible set, or a tuple outside its context's set, shall be rejected (`evidence_lint` LINT-AUTH-03, fail-closed); an UNREGISTERED well-formed method is accepted, preserved verbatim and treated as **unassessed** (LINT-AUTH-W1 — the safe-generic-processing convention). `n/a` is admissible in no evidence context of this clause. **Transport-commitment issuance (normative).** The sender wallet shall submit the **exact transmitted `MLSMessage` octets together with the submission metadata**; RDP(out) shall **compute** `envelope_hash` from those octets and shall not copy a sender-supplied value — a supplied commitment that disagrees shall cause a typed `envelope-hash-mismatch` rejection **before any evidence is sealed**. RDP(out) shall hand the same octets to the Delivery Service, so the bytes attested and the bytes transported are one object. For `mls_state`, RDP(out) shall obtain the **retained GroupContext** for the referenced `(mls_group_id, mls_epoch)` from the Delivery Service and hash it itself; SE therefore records an RDP-verified state, not a sender claim. Idempotency binds the `message_id` to those octets: replaying it with different bytes shall be rejected at both services. Before this rule the submission carried only a claimed digest while the bytes reached the Delivery Service by a separate call with no normative binding — RDP(out) sealed an attestation over octets it never received, and a different message could be delivered under the same metadata.

**The idempotency namespace is the issuing provider's (normative).** The global handle is `(issuing-RDP identity, message_id)`, and both services shall key idempotency on that pair — never on `message_id` alone, which put every provider in one namespace: the same identifier from two providers collided, and one provider could displace another's binding. **The issuing identity shall be derived from the authenticated principal** — except for a submission a recipient-side RDP FORWARDS to its Delivery Service, whose originating namespace shall be proven by the originating provider's sealed SE for that `message_id` and those octets, the forwarder being recorded beside it — not read from the request: a client-supplied field would have to be checked against the principal in any case, so carrying it adds a spoofing surface without adding information. **The derivation is defined, not left to the deployment.** Message submission is authenticated by the issuing RDP's own credential — a scheme distinct from the member credential, because a member token identifies a participant of a federation entity and carries no RDP identity — and `rdp_id` is taken from the presented client certificate: exactly one `subjectAltName` URI of the canonical form `urn:sbm:rdp:<uid>` — none, or more than one, shall be refused (the Delivery Service contract's `rdpAuth` scheme states the rule, and the reference derives it in `lint_cli`; this clause references it rather than restating it). *This clause used to permit a fallback to the subject DN's `organizationIdentifier` (2.5.4.97), which a later round removed everywhere else: it identifies a LEGAL ENTITY, one entity may operate several RDPs, and the fallback collapsed them into one namespace — the collision the namespace rule exists to prevent.* A certificate yielding no canonical identifier shall be refused, since an RDP whose identity cannot be derived has no namespace to be idempotent within and would silently share one. *Before this the operation was bound to the member credential, declared as a bearer token with a description offering mutual TLS as an alternative — so a conforming generated client presented a member token for the one operation whose identity this clause makes load-bearing.* The same `message_id` from a **different** authenticated provider is a different message and shall not collide; from the **same** provider with different octets it is `duplicate-message-id`; with the same octets it returns the same record. An unauthenticated submission has no namespace to be idempotent within and shall be refused.

**The acceptance record is live transaction evidence (normative).** It is consumed by RDP(out) during the submission. It is not signed, not retained and not bound into the evidence model, so a later verifier shall not be expected to correlate it; what a later verifier correlates is the **sealed SE**, whose `envelope_hash` RDP(out) computed from the same octets the Delivery Service accepted. Its integrity therefore comes from the **authenticated channel** it arrives on, which shall be required.

**Submission is one transaction, in one order (normative).** The transport-commitment rule fixed WHAT RDP(out) attests; it did not state WHEN, and the wallet→RDP and RDP→DS operations were independent with no ordering, durable state, recovery or response proof between them. RDP(out) shall: (1) validate the octets and **durably reserve** the `(message_id → envelope_hash)` binding; (2) hand the SAME octets to the Delivery Service and obtain an **acceptance record** echoing `(message_id, mls_group_id, envelope_hash)`, where the Delivery Service **computes** the digest from what it received rather than echoing the submitter's; and only then (3) seal SE. **Sealing before acceptance shall be prohibited** — an SE attests that bytes were accepted for transport, and one sealed earlier attests something that has not happened and may never happen. Recovery is defined at each interruption point: before acceptance no SE exists and the retry converges on the reserved binding; after acceptance the acceptance is idempotent and the retry seals against the same record; after sealing the retry returns the SAME SE (INTF-4). Both services shall reject a `message_id` already bound to different octets, independently of each other, since a Delivery Service serving several providers cannot rely on one of them having checked. The observable invariant is **one `message_id`, one byte binding, at most one SE, at most one delivery**.

**Grade-mismatch dispute — proof of attribution (normative).** A GCM-v1 dispute shall carry a **recipient-produced `reveal_confirmation`**: the disputing member's device wallet advanced electronic signature over `(message_id, envelope_hash, grade_commitment, salt, content_class, read_at, recipient_uid, mid, device_id)`, verified against that device's published `confirmation_key` **as it stood at `read_at`** (INTF-1/1a/2, the as-of read). A dispute without such a confirmation, or whose signed tuple does not cover the disputed values, shall be rejected: **commitment inequality alone proves nothing**, because inequality is manufacturable by inventing a salt — before this rule, any recipient provider could rebut any availability-grade delivery. The salt shall be exactly 16 bytes (32 lowercase hexadecimal characters) and malformed input shall yield a typed validation failure, never an unhandled error in the verifier. **Scope of the proof (stated plainly):** the confirmation establishes an **attributable recipient assertion** — a named device of a named member is accountable for the asserted values — and does **not** establish that those values were extracted from the disputed ciphertext. A member may still assert a false reveal; the protocol makes that act attributable and disputable (the accountability log, §13.2 dispute path) rather than impossible. Objective ciphertext-to-reveal proof would require a construction this profile does not define and is recorded as **future work** (prerequisites: a commitment scheme binding the decrypted envelope to the reveal, verifiable by a third party without the plaintext). The Article 43(2) residual (D5) is unchanged and separate.

**Welcome collection is device-bound (normative).** A Welcome carries the group secrets of ONE device, so its **collection** shall require device-bound authentication; an entity- or member-level credential shall not collect it, and shall not be able to collect the secrets of every device it covers. **Deposit** is authorised separately and remains member-level, since handing a Welcome to a device is not taking possession of its secrets. A device shall reach only its own queue, and an item belonging to another device shall be indistinguishable from an unknown item — a distinct error would confirm that another device has a Welcome pending, which is an existence oracle over that device's state.

**Availability-grade delivery gating (normative).** Where the recipient entity has declared availability-grade delivery for a content class (umbrella §8.3b), the delivery point shall be an **authenticated recipient endpoint**: a device enrolled to the recipient entity under this clause, holding the message group's MLS keys, acting within a session authenticated by the recipient's provider with an approved `recipient_auth_method`. The RDP shall issue the availability-grade DE (`delivery_grade` = `availability`, `event` = `D.1-ContentConsignment`) only upon the **acknowledged handover** (the I-D delivery-state model): the message bytes transferred to such an endpoint AND the session transport's receipt acknowledgement received — the Delivery-Service **signed receipt** — binding (message_id, recipient_uid, mid, device_id, session_binding, server_time, message_digest) — is the protocol proof, shall be retained for the evidence-retention period, and `delivered_at` shall be the receipt's `server_time`. **The event instant shall be observed by the Delivery Service:** a client-supplied timestamp shall not determine it and, where retained, is non-authoritative diagnostic metadata; the Delivery Service shall authenticate the acknowledging **device** for this operation (an entity- or member-level credential shall not satisfy it); and the RDP shall verify the receipt's signature and its `message_digest` against the octets it committed to at submission before issuing the DE. An authenticated session whose queue merely holds the message shall NOT be treated as delivery; the first acknowledged handover per message is the event and later acknowledgements are idempotent (no second DE). The RDP shall record the session's `recipient_auth_method` and `auth_context` in the DE; availability to an unauthenticated store, queue or mailbox shall not be treated as delivery at any grade. An availability-grade DE shall carry `integrity_basis` = `sender-declared-digest` and shall not carry a recipient confirmation object: it attests the availability of the sealed content to an authenticated endpoint of the identified addressee, and the sender-declared digest; it shall not be read as attesting recipient-side verification of content integrity, nor acceptance. The sender wallet computes and supplies the **grade commitment** with the submission (the I-D, *Grade Commitment*); the RDP shall echo it verbatim into SE and into the availability-grade DE as `grade_commitment`, and shall not issue an availability-grade DE without one.

**Article 44(1)(c) — how identification before delivery is ensured at the availability grade (normative statement and rationale).** Identification of the addressee before the delivery of the data is ensured at credential and session level: the addressee entity is identified by the qualified issuance of its UID attestation / QSealC (this clause, first paragraph); its members and devices are bound to the entity by the BW-MEMBER enrolment; and availability-grade delivery occurs exclusively into an authenticated session of such a bound device. The recipient-authentication gating of CIR (EU) 2025/1944 (REQ-QERDS-5.2.2-03A) maps to that authenticated retrieval session: the addressee endpoint is authenticated **before** the data is delivered to it, and the DE records how. The rule of this clause that mere device acknowledgement of an MLS PrivateMessage shall not on its own satisfy Article 44(1)(c) is unaffected: possession of group keys or a decryption event without an authenticated provider session identifies no addressee — the availability anchor is the authenticated session, never key possession.

NOTE: Mailbox-based registered systems anchor legal effect at deposit into a provider-side mailbox whose holder is identified contractually. The availability grade anchors **later and stronger**: at handover into an authenticated session of a credential-bound device of the addressee. Relative to the verification and acceptance grades, what is relaxed is the recipient **confirmation** (the integrity attestation, `s3_attestation`); the identification and authentication of the addressee — the Article 44(1)(c) substance — remain fully gated.

**Grade-commitment reveal (dispute procedure, normative).** The declared-class binding of an availability-grade DE is verifiable without trusting the issuer. In a dispute, either party reveals `(salt, content_class)` — the salt travels only in the end-to-end-encrypted envelope, so both endpoints hold it. Any verifier shall then (a) recompute the commitment per the I-D construction against the BW-ORG version referenced by the evidence (`acceptance_policy_ref.doc_digest`) and check equality with the sealed `grade_commitment`, and (b) check that the revealed `content_class` maps to `availability` in that BW-ORG's `delivery_grades`. What each outcome ESTABLISHES, stated separately. **A matching reveal** of a declared class establishes, cryptographically and for any verifier, that the commitment opens to that class. **A failing reveal** establishes nothing on its own: commitment inequality is manufacturable by inventing a salt (the proof-of-attribution clause above). What a recipient can produce is an **attributable assertion** — its signed reveal inside a GCM, naming the member and device accountable for the asserted values — and that assertion **initiates a dispute**; it does not settle one. Whether the availability-grade DE may then be relied upon is decided through the dispute path (umbrella §13.2), not by the reveal. *This clause used to say a failing reveal lets the recipient "establish misuse" and that the DE "shall not be relied upon", contradicting the clause above, which says inequality alone proves nothing.* Where the recipient asserts such a mismatch on reading, its RDP shall issue a **GCM-v1** grade-commitment-mismatch evidence object ([I-D], *Grade-Commitment Mismatch evidence*) carrying the reveal and the disputed commitment; a valid GCM **rebuts** the availability-grade DE in dispute without retracting it. *[**TODO(legal):** the Article 43(2) final-but-rebuttable clause awaits external counsel (D5).]* A reveal discloses one message's content class to the parties of that dispute and nothing else.

**Mandate-commitment reveal (dispute procedure, normative, umbrella Annex R / A1).** An **opposable** agent SE (`auth_context.identity` = system, `mandate_ref.opposable` ≠ false) shall carry a `mandate_commitment` (the I-D, *Mandate Commitment*); the RDP shall not accept an opposable agent submission without one. The commitment binds the acted-under mandate to the message's `content_class` without either leaking **from the commitment itself** (the public `mandate_ref` and the BW-MEMBER scope mapping retain their documented exposure). In a dispute, either party reveals `(salt, content_class)` — the salt travels only in the end-to-end-encrypted envelope. Any verifier shall then (a) recompute the commitment per the I-D construction against the referenced BW-ORG version (`acceptance_policy_ref.doc_digest`) and check equality with the sealed `mandate_commitment`, and (b) check that the revealed `content_class` is **in the acting member's mandate scope** (`BW-MEMBER.mandate_ref.scope`). A matching reveal of an in-scope class shows that the class the sender **committed to** lies within the mandate's scope; a reveal of an out-of-scope class establishes that the **declaration** was outside it — **overreach in the declared act**. Neither establishes what the encrypted document actually was: the commitment binds the sender's declaration, and no party to the reveal inspects the plaintext. An in-scope opening therefore does not certify the agent's actual conduct, and an opening that fails to verify establishes neither misuse nor overreach — only that the reveal does not match the commitment. This is the same limit clause 6 states for the grade commitment. Where an agent SE is marked `opposable: false` it carries no commitment and mandate-scope conformance is establishable only by internal audit, payload reveal, or the dispute path — never by external verification (`bundle_lint` LINT-BND-15 with a reveal fixture; `evidence_lint` LINT-DE-15).

## 7 Change indication (Article 44(1)(e))

Under end-to-end encryption the RDP/MSP never sees plaintext, so content transformation is forbidden, and an intermediary cannot transform the end-to-end-encrypted envelope at all. Any permitted transformation would therefore be on the envelope/metadata — and **neither of the two the profile enumerates (`re-packaging`, `chunking`) is profiled**: an RDP **shall not** issue a CE with either until the operation and the commitment over its outputs are defined (the I-D [I-D], *Application Envelope*; review agenda A15). What follows describes the CE's shape and duty, which stand, for the transformations a later revision defines. An MLS **epoch change re-encrypts no queued application message**: a sender that resubmits after one makes a **new submission**, with a new SE and a new evidence chain, and not a transformation of the queued one — the epoch-change CE type was removed from the profile and `schemas/evidence-ce.schema.json` does not admit it. Whenever a transformation the profile has defined occurs — none is defined today — the responsible RDP shall issue a Change-Indication Evidence (CE) recording the `transformation` type and `changed_at`, sealed and carrying a qualified timestamp. The CE shall be made available to both the sender and the addressee and should be referenced from the EP `changes[]`.

**DE event selection.** A DE shall set `event` to `E.1-ContentHandover` (default), `D.6-ContentAccessTracking`, `C.3-ConsignmentAcceptance` (S4 under quorum/all), or `D.1-ContentConsignment` (the declared availability grade only, clause 6). `B.1` (S1) is an ancillary state event carried in `EP.states[]`, never a DE `event`; `D.1` is a DE `event` only at the declared availability grade, otherwise an ancillary state record.

**Acceptance within scope.** Where a message is sent in a confidentiality scope (umbrella §8.3a), acceptance — including quorum — shall be evaluated over the scope's members only, i.e. the leaves of the (entity pair, scope) group, not over the entity-wide roster (SCOPE-8).

**Reason-to-event binding (normative, SCOPE-9/REASON-3).** An NDE `event` shall be consistent with its `reason` (clause 8.1; `evidence_lint` LINT-NDE-07): `expired` ⇒ C.5; `malformed-envelope`/`policy-block`/`quota-exceeded`/`unknown-uid`/`unsupported-protocol`/`no-matching-scope`/`scope-violation` (pre-submission) ⇒ A.2; `recipient-unreachable`/`mls-group-invalid`/`payload-hash-mismatch` ⇒ D.2; `routing-failed` ⇒ B.3 when relay to the counterpart provider fails, or D.2 when consignment to the recipient fails. `scope-violation` detected **before submission** is an NDE with A.2; a recipient's **explicit refusal** of an out-of-scope content class is a Refusal Evidence (RE), which is C.4-ConsignmentRejection by type.

Justified deviations: C.1/C.2/C.6 (notify-for-acceptance) — MLS store-and-forward has no separate step; D.3/D.4/D.5 and F.1/F.2/F.3 — out of scope for the all-ERDS ecosystem.

## 9 Conformance profiles

A deployment shall operate under exactly one profile; evidence indicates it via the `profile` field, and the applicable profile is observable from the RDP's Trusted-List and federation-membership status.

**Pilot profile.** RDPs may operate under a transitional regime where QERDS qualification is not yet required, provided the pilot is limited in scope, evidence is marked non-qualified (`profile`="pilot"), and RDPs commit to obtaining QERDS qualification before production. A pilot deployment shall not present its evidence as qualified or claim the Article 43(2) presumption.

**Production (qualified) profile.** RDPs are QERDS-qualified and admitted to the federation; evidence (`profile`="production") carries the Article 44 guarantees and the Article 43(2) presumption. `production` is a **claim**: a verifier shall not treat the string alone as qualification; it establishes the presumption only after validating, in conjunction, the Trusted-List status of `rdp_id`, federation membership, the certificate chain and object-level seal, `policy_id`, and the qualified timestamp. NOTE: An informative, verifier-shaped assembly of these obligations — including what the companion repository's demo validators do *not* establish — is provided in the companion document `docs/production-verifier-architecture.md`.

| Aspect | Pilot | Production |
|---|---|---|
| RDP qualification | not yet required (transitional) | QERDS-qualified, Trusted-List-inscribed |
| Evidence marking | `profile`="pilot" | `profile`="production" |
| Article 43(2) presumption | not claimed | claimed |
| Key transparency | not required | roadmap target — mechanism not yet profiled (NOTE) |
| KeyPackage lifetime maximum | 90 days | 30 days |
| Four-corner relay | optional, uncertified | CAB-confirmed before interop claims |
| Long-term validation | RECOMMENDED | REQUIRED for archived evidence |

> **NOTE (key transparency — roadmap, not a delivered control).** Key transparency is the intended production mitigation for discovery-key substitution by an equivocating directory, but this profile does **not** yet specify a mechanism: no log structure, logged-object schema, inclusion/consistency/freshness proofs, gossip, auditor role, client verification algorithm or failure behaviour is defined. It is a **roadmap** item — the Internet-Draft Security Considerations treat it as "a profile matter". Until a concrete mechanism is profiled, conformance does **not** require it and the discovery-key-substitution threat is **not** claimed closed. Its normative specification (and any qualification-relevant wording) is deferred. *[**TODO(legal):** the qualification interaction of a mandated transparency control is an external-counsel item.]*
